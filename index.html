When does a chemical anomaly favour technology?

Change the likelihoods and prior weights, then see how the Chemical Improbability Principle and Silence Threshold respond. The numbers are generated for exploration. They are not measured astrochemistry.

CIP calibration lab

A reproducible, deliberately small implementation of the Bayesian comparison in Paper III. Choose a fictional model, generate repeated observations, and inspect the decision boundary.

Model assumptions
Probability of one defined feature in one synthetic observation
Scope: The simulation uses a single binary feature, fixed likelihoods, independent trials, and user-visible prior weights. Random assumptions are sampled from stated demonstration ranges. This is not a calibrated chemical model; environmental conditioning, parameter marginalization, the Paper II CAI, and Paper I are not implemented. No entered data leaves this browser.

What the calculation means

The posterior probability for each explanation is proportional to its likelihood multiplied by its prior. The displayed CIP is the log posterior support for technology relative to the two natural explanations combined. A high value does not prove technology.

The Silence Threshold is the selected upper-tail quantile of simulated natural and unresolved-natural cases. The reported false-positive rate is measured against that same synthetic reference population. With finite samples and tied values, it may differ slightly from α.

The implementation follows the equations proposed in Paper III. The distributions and feature values in this browser demonstration are illustrative assumptions, not parameters specified or calibrated by the paper.
